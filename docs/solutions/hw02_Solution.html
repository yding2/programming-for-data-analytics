Part II. Cars Data (45 points)

We’ll look at data frame and plotting in much more detail in later classes. For a preview of what’s to come, here’s a very basic example.

For this example we’ll use a very simple dataset. The cars data comes with the default installation of R. To see the first few rows of the data, just type head(cars).

head(cars)
  speed dist
1     4    2
2     4   10
3     7    4
4     7   22
5     8   16
6     9   10

(a) Use summary() to summarize cars. Report the minimum, maximum, mean, and median of both speed and dist. (10 points)

summary(cars)
     speed           dist       
 Min.   : 4.0   Min.   :  2.00  
 1st Qu.:12.0   1st Qu.: 26.00  
 Median :15.0   Median : 36.00  
 Mean   :15.4   Mean   : 42.98  
 3rd Qu.:19.0   3rd Qu.: 56.00  
 Max.   :25.0   Max.   :120.00

The minimal value of speed is 4, maximal value of speed is 25, mean value of speed is 15.4, and meadian value of speed is 15. The minimal value of dist is 2, maximal value of dist is 120, mean value of dist is 42.98, and meadian value of dist is 36.

(b) Calculate the variance and standard deviation of speed and dist (5 points)

var_speed <- var(cars$speed)
var_speed
[1] 27.95918
std_speed <- sd(cars$speed)
std_speed
[1] 5.287644
var_dist <- var(cars$dist)
var_dist
[1] 664.0608
std_dist <- sd(cars$dist)
std_dist
[1] 25.76938

The variance of speed is 27.9591837. The standard deviation of speed is 5.2876444. The variance of dist is 664.0608163. The standard deviation of dist is 25.7693775.

(c) Calculate the mean and standard deviation of dist for observations where speed is greater than 12 and less than 19. (5 points)

selected.dist <- cars$dist[cars$speed > 12 & cars$speed < 19]

mean(selected.dist)
[1] 44.7
sd(selected.dist)
[1] 18.61833

The average of dist when speed is greater than 12 and less than 19 is 44.7. The standard deviation is 18.6183.

(d) Produce a histogram of speed with 10 breaks. Please illustrate your observation from the output. (5 points)

hist(cars$speed,
     breaks = seq(min(cars$speed), max(cars$speed), length.out = 11),
     main = "Histogram of Speed",
     xlab = "Speed",
     col = "lightblue",
     border = "white")

Most speeds fall between approximately 10 and 20, with the largest concentration near the middle of this range. The distribution is roughly unimodal and does not appear to be strongly skewed.

(e) Produce a histogram of dist with 20 breaks. Please illustrate your observation from the output. (5 points)

hist(cars$dist,
     breaks = seq(min(cars$dist), max(cars$dist), length.out = 21),
     main = "Histogram of Stopping Distance",
     xlab = "Stopping Distance",
     col = "lightgreen",
     border = "white")

Most stopping distances are relatively small or moderate, while a few observations have much larger distances. The distribution is right-skewed.

(f) Use the boxplot function to create a boxplot of speed. Briefly describe the center, spread, and any possible outliers. (5 points)

boxplot(cars$speed,
        main = "Boxplot of Speed",
        ylab = "Speed",
        col = "lightblue")
boxplot.stats(cars$speed)$stats
[1]  4 12 15 19 25
boxplot.stats(cars$speed)$out
numeric(0)

The median speed is 15. The middle 50% of the speeds extend from 12 to 19, so the IQR is 7. No observations are identified as possible outliers by the boxplot rule.

(g) Use the boxplot function to create a boxplot of dist. Please illustrate your observation from the output. (5 points)

boxplot(cars$dist,
        main = "Boxplot of Stopping Distance",
        ylab = "Stopping Distance",
        col = "lightgreen")
boxplot.stats(cars$dist)$stats
[1]  2 26 36 56 93
boxplot.stats(cars$dist)$out
[1] 120

The median stopping distance is 36. The middle 50% of the distances extend from 26 to 56, so the IQR is 30. The value 120 is shown as a possible outlier above the upper whisker. The longer upper portion of the plot is consistent with a right-skewed distribution.

(h) Use qqnorm and qqline to investigate whether dist is approximately normally distributed. Briefly explain your conclusion. (5 points)

qqnorm(cars$dist,
       main = "Normal Q-Q Plot of Stopping Distance")
qqline(cars$dist, col = "red", lwd = 2)

If dist were approximately normally distributed, the points would lie close to the reference line. The points deviate noticeably from the line, especially in the upper tail. Together with the right-skewed histogram and the high outlier, this suggests that dist is not approximately normally distributed.

Part IV. Normal Distributions and Critical Values (35 points)

Question 1. A soft-drink machine is regulated so that it discharges an average of 200 milliliters per cup. If the amount of drink is normally distributed with a standard deviation equal to 15 milliliters,

(a) what fraction of the cups will contain more than 224 milliliters? (5 points)

pmt224 <- pnorm(224, mean = 200, sd = 15, lower.tail = FALSE)
pmt224
[1] 0.05479929

The fraction of the cups will contain more than 224 milliliters is 0.0547993.

(b) what is the probability that a cup contains between 191 and 209 milliliters? (5 points)

pbd <- pnorm(209, mean = 200, sd = 15) - pnorm(191, mean = 200, sd = 15)
pbd
[1] 0.4514938

The probability that a cup contains between 191 and 209 milliliters 0.4514938.

(c) below what value do we get the smallest 25% of the drinks? (5 points)

q25 <- qnorm(0.25, mean = 200, sd = 15)
q25
[1] 189.8827

Below 189.8826537 we get the smallest 25% of the drinks.

Question 2. Critical values with qnorm

(a) Please use qnorm to find the critical values \(z_{0.1}\). (5 points)

alpha <- 0.1
zalpha <- qnorm(1 - 0.10)
zalpha
[1] 1.281552

\(z_{0.1}\) is 1.2815516.

(b) Find the value of \(z_0\) such that \(90\%\) of the standard normal \(Z\) values lie between \(-z_0\) and \(z_0\); that is, \(P(-z_0\leq Z\leq z_0) = .90\). (10 points)

alpha <- 1 - 0.90
z0 <- qnorm(1 - alpha / 2)
z0
[1] 1.644854

\(z_{0}\) is 1.6448536.

(c) Which value is larger? Why? (5 points)

Here \(z_{0}\) is \(z_{0.05}\), which should be larger than \(z_{0.1}\). Because \(P(Z \le z_{0.05}) = 1-0.05 = 0.95 > 0.9 = 1-0.1 = P(Z \le z_{0.1})\).
